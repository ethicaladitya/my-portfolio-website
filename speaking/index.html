01

Beyond Chatbots: Building AI Agents That Actually Do Things

What turns an LLM into an agent? Tool calling, MCP, memory, planning, and agent loops, shown through real developer workflows: inspecting Linux, calling APIs, working with WordPress, and reading logs. Plus the lesson most demos skip: when a shell script or Ansible playbook is still the better answer.

  • 35–40 min talk
  • DevFest, GDG, developer conferences

02

I Gave an AI Agent SSH Access: Here's What I Learned

AI agents on a real Linux server: gathering context over SSH, investigating high CPU, debugging WordPress, spotting suspicious files. Then the hard part: permission boundaries, command restrictions, secrets, human approval, audit logs, and rollback.

  • 30–40 min talk
  • WordCamps, DevOps & cloud meetups

03

Security Isn't a Plugin: Lessons From Real WordPress Incidents

Stories from real compromised sites: the reused password, the patch that came too late, the "free" premium plugin, the developer's laptop. No fear-mongering and no product pitch, just what actually stops the next incident.

  • 20–30 min talk
  • WordCamps, WordPress meetups, agencies

04

WP-CLI Unleashed

Managing WordPress from the command line: bulk operations, search-replace, migrations, and scripting routine work. Delivered at WordCamp Asia 2023 and WordCamp Nepal 2022; available as a hands-on workshop.

Short bio · 51 words

Aditya Shah is a Hosting Support Manager and DevOps Engineer at WPMU DEV and has organized the WordPress Bhopal community since 2015. In 11 years of speaking at WordCamps, colleges, and developer events, he has reached 50,000+ people with talks on WordPress security, WP-CLI, performance, and AI agents for real operations.

Long bio · 142 words

Aditya Shah leads hosting support at WPMU DEV, where he manages a distributed team and stays hands-on with Linux infrastructure, incident response, WordPress security investigations, and automation. He has worked with WordPress since 2014, founded and still runs the WordPress Bhopal community (100+ meetups and events since 2015), helped run the WP Build Tour that introduced 1,500+ students to WordPress, and is part of the WordCamp Asia 2026 organizing team. He has spoken at WordCamp Asia, WordCamp Nepal, WordCamp Pokhara, WordCamp Kochi, and WordCamp Mumbai, at Build with AI by Google & Hack2Skill, and at many college events on WordPress, Linux, DNS, and how websites work. In 11 years of speaking he has reached more than 50,000 people. He also contributes to WP-CLI. He loves helping people, and his talks turn production lessons into practical takeaways audiences can use the following Monday.
